learn three crypto tricks

A Caesar cipher shifts every letter by the key. Anyone can crack it by trying all 26 keys, so real ciphers use far bigger keys.

Alice and Bob pick private numbers and swap results in public. They still end up with the same secret.

This is the Diffie–Hellman idea with tiny numbers. A spy sees only the swapped numbers. With huge numbers, working backward is too slow to be practical.
This is SHA-256 (first 24 characters). Change one letter and the whole fingerprint changes. Fingerprints check files and protect stored passwords.

so, did the promise hold?

The words stayed. The practice moved.

The 1986 law still says interception needs legal authority. The files show how exceptions, secret legal readings, and weak links in the network widened what was collected. Encryption became the tool users and companies reached for in response, and lawmakers are still arguing over who gets a key.
